Why US auctions and why now

US auctions — Copart and IAAI — offer the largest inventory, transparent information (VIN, images, title type) and competitive prices. With proper preparation (budget definition, deep VIN checks, bidding strategy), you often get a better price‑to‑value ratio than on the local market. At the same time, without the right partner risk and final cost can rise. Experience, attention to detail and an upfront all‑in calculation are critical.

Complete cost structure — what forms the total

The all‑in budget usually consists of the following components:

  1. Winning Bid (Auction Price) — the actual sale price at auction.
  2. Auction Fees — Buyer Fee, Internet Bid Fee, Gate Fee and other platform charges.
  3. Inland Transport (USA) — moving the vehicle from yard to port/warehouse.
  4. Ocean Freight — container.
  5. Marine Insurance — ocean insurance (free with us)
  6. Port/Handling — terminal services and port fees.
  7. Georgia Inland — Poti/Batumi → Tbilisi (or other city).
  8. Customs (clearance) — per current legal framework.

Copart/IAAI — price and fees (Auction Fees)

Auction fees depend on the winning bid range. The structure is progressive: the higher the sale price, the higher the Buyer Fee. Internet Bid Fee and Gate Fee also matter. Each platform (Copart/IAAI) periodically updates its tables, so a prior calculation is necessary for each specific lot.

  • Buyer Fee: main fee, depends on the price band.
  • Internet Bid Fee: online bidding surcharge.
  • Gate/Storage: yard service/storage (if pickup is delayed).

In practice, Copart vs IAAI differences show up in slightly different fee ladders, yard policies, inventory types and sometimes availability of Buy It Now/Make Offer modes.

Inland transport in the USA — from yard to port

Inland transport pricing depends on distance, drivable status, vehicle size/weight and pickup SLA. Run & Drive vehicles are typically cheaper to move; non‑runners cost more.

Insurance — when and why

Insurance isn’t mandatory, but it’s recommended for high‑value, rare or special vehicles. In case of a claim you’ll need photos, invoices, Bill of Lading and a damage report. Insurance reduces financial risk. (With us, marine insurance is free.)

Routes and ports — Savannah/Newark/Baltimore → Poti/Batumi

  • Savannah → Poti/Batumi — frequent sailings, competitive container pricing.
  • Newark → Poti/Batumi — for loads from Northeast yards.

We choose the optimal route per lot. The goal is balance: price × time × risk.

Customs in Georgia — what determines the tax

Customs depends on engine displacement (cm³), fuel type (gas/diesel/hybrid/electric), vehicle age (model year), eco‑class and the current legal framework. Rules can change; we calculate precisely for the specific lot on the selection day. An upfront “whole‑picture” estimate protects you from surprises.

  2. Bidding — depends on the auction calendar/request.

Bidding strategy — Buy It Now vs Live Auction

BIN (Buy It Now) is great when time is critical and the budget is set; Live Auction can save money but increases the risk that the price exceeds your planned ceiling.

  • Market research: review sold data by year/model/trim.
  • Damage type: front/rear/side/hail/mechanical — each has different risk/price.
  • Title type: Clean/Salvage/Rebuilt/Export Only — legal and financial differences.
  • Geography: yard location influences inland transport.
  • Discipline: a predefined all‑in cap is the key lever against “emotional bidding”.

VIN/Carfax/AutoCheck and titles (Clean/Salvage/Rebuilt/Export Only)

The VIN (Vehicle Identification Number) is your main tool. Carfax/AutoCheck reveal ownership, service, accidents, mileage, and registrations. Title type shapes both the price and legal framework.

  • Clean: usually best, but doesn’t guarantee a “clean” history — photo analysis still needed.
  • Salvage: damaged, often good value if repair is budgeted correctly.
  • Rebuilt: restored — verify quality.
  • Export Only: export only; cannot be registered in the US; registration in Georgia is possible per law.

Common mistakes and how to avoid them
