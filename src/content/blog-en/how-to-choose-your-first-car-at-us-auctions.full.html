Why auctions and where to start

US auctions provide the best balance of price × choice × transparency. Photos, VIN, title and notes are public, and competition reduces excessive markup. To begin, define your scenario (city/family/hobby), then list your must‑have and nice‑to‑have criteria — this prevents unnecessary compromise.

Budget and the All‑In approach

The first mistake beginners make is focusing only on the “lot price”. In reality you care about the all‑in budget:

  • auction price + fees (Buyer Fee, Internet Bid Fee, Gate/Storage)
  • inland transport (USA) — yard to port
  • container — booking/consolidation
  • port — services/terminal
  • inland transport in Georgia — Poti/Batumi → Tbilisi/other
  • customs — displacement/age/eco‑class
  • buffer — 5–10% contingency

Criteria: real need → correct model

  • Usage: city/highways/road conditions.
  • Fuel: gas/diesel/hybrid/electric — budget and infrastructure factors.
  • Size/cargo: sedan vs crossover/SUV, boot space/child seats.
  • Displacement and tax factor: impacts customs.
  • Service/parts: common models have cheap and available parts.
  • Safety/ADAS: sensors/cameras/calibration — raises repair costs.
  • Liquidity: ease of resale later.

Research: VIN, Carfax/AutoCheck and history

The VIN is your “passport”. Carfax/AutoCheck reports show service entries, accidents, registrations, verified mileage and sometimes photos. Pay attention to the chronology: multiple accidents in short succession or frequent region changes are a risk. Regular oil intervals and official service are a good sign.

Damage types — what they really mean

  • Front/Rear/Side: body panels, headlights, radiator, bumpers, sensors.
  • Hail: visual damage — often cheap to win, but needs proper restoration.
  • Mechanical: engine/transmission — risky without diagnostics.
  • Water/Flood: the riskiest — electrics, sensors, corrosion, smell. “Clean” visuals don’t guarantee safety.
  • Bio/chemical: rare, special disinfection/storage protocols.
  • Minor dents/scratches: straightforward restoration.

“Run & Drive” does not mean fully roadworthy — only that the car starts and moves within the yard. Airbag deployed? — safety system and interior add cost. Frame damage/twist — high risk.

Title types — legal framework

  • Clean: generally best, but still needs verification.
  • Salvage: damaged status; can be great value if repair is calculated realistically.
  • Rebuilt: restored; quality varies — verification is critical.
  • Export Only: export‑only; cannot be registered in the US; registration in Georgia is legal per regulations.

The title determines not only price but also procedures. Each type has a slightly different transport/document path — we manage the process to simplify it for you.

Photo analysis — what we actually see

Evaluate auction photos with these criteria:

  • Panel gaps and cracks: uneven gaps suggest structural issues.
  • Radiator: with a front hit — cracked/bent radiator support is often costly.
  • Underbody: if visible — corrosion/leaks? water/oil drips.
  • Damaged bumper/headlight: purely cosmetic or structural behind?
  • Airbag signs: steering/dash airbags deployed.
  • Cabin signs: water lines, mold spots, smell — flood risk.
  • Tires: uneven wear — suspension/geometry issues.
  • Engine bay: wiring, non‑OEM parts — traces of prior repair.
  • ODO/Key: key status, mileage, cluster warnings.

Specific watchouts

Each segment/model has typical “end cost” patterns. Before the final choice, check Recall/TSB lists, service and parts costs. This helps forecast realistic repair and ownership costs.

  • Direct Injection: carbon buildup — plan periodic cleaning.
  • Turbo engines: oil consumption/cooling issues; check TSB/recalls.
  • Hybrid/Electric: HV battery, inverter, thermal management.
  • AWD/4x4: mismatched tires hurt differentials/transfer case — visible in photos too.
  • ADAS sensors: after bumper/windshield replacement calibration is required — a cost factor.
  • Headlights/grilles/trim: rare trims can be expensive — pre‑search catalogs.

Inspection: remote and on‑site

You can learn a lot remotely too: video/additional photos are sometimes available. In special cases we use local inspectors (market‑based), but the cost should be proportional to lot value. For high‑value/rare models inspection is recommended.

Repair cost: how to estimate realistically

The exact cost is visible only after disassembly, but you can still build an upfront “whole picture”:

  • Body: hood/bumper/headlight — parts × paint × labor.
  • Frame/geometry: frame bench/calibration — can be the most expensive.
  • Safety: airbags, sensors, OEM calibration — ADAS especially.
  • Engine/transmission: unknown noises/codes — never “assume” without diagnostics.
  • Hybrid/electric: HV battery, inverter, coolant — specialist assessment.

We help with catalogs and experience. Often “less damaged but pricier lot” beats a “very cheap, deep‑damage” car — in the end the former is more reasonable.

Pre‑Bid checklist

  • Set your all‑in maximum (with buffer) — and don’t exceed it.
  • Verify VIN reports and photos — at least 2 sources.
  • Assess title and damage — legal/technical risk.
  • Clarify need: city/family/fuel/safety.
  • Agree with Swift on bidding day/cap/alternatives.

Bidding strategy — Buy It Now vs Live Auction

Buy It Now (BIN): quick close when the price fits your all‑in cap. Saves time.
Live Auction: chance for a lower lot price, but emotional risk. A predefined maximum is critical.

  • Use a proxy bid up to your max and don’t follow spontaneous raises.
  • Read auction notes carefully: “as‑is where‑is” — all risk is on the buyer.
  • Re‑check fee tables right before bidding.

Make Offer — when it’s worth it

Make Offer helps when BIN is overpriced or a lot has been sitting too long. Prepare: VIN history, recent sales range (sold data) and readiness for quick payment (settling the invoice).

  • Timing: the day of sale or within the next 24–48 hours works best.
  • Range: send a band instead of a single number; increases acceptance chances.
  • Argument: title/damage/liquidity + your all‑in cap — short and specific.
  • Discipline: never exceed the predefined maximum.

We negotiate to keep your maximum protected and to prepare documents/invoice quickly.

Ownership costs (TCO)

The right choice is not defined by purchase price alone. Consider fuel, service/parts, parking/size. Some models cost more upfront but need less service.

  • Fuel: in city use — hybrids have an advantage.
  • Storage/parking: large SUVs cost more in the city.
  • Service/parts: popular models are cheaper and faster to service.
  • Price: Japanese sedans/crossovers historically retain value well.
  • Insurance: powerful/sporty cars increase premiums.

We can compare TCO across options in advance so your choice remains optimal over 3–5 years.

Risk control and hidden costs

  • Yard Storage: delayed pickup increases costs — deadline control matters.
  • Port Demurrage/Detention: port waiting fees — fast documentation reduces them.
  • Hidden Damage: invisible in photos; hence the 5–10% buffer.
  • Calibration/ADAS: modern system calibration can be expensive.

US locations and yards — where and why price changes

The USA inland cost and competition depend on yard location. Yards near ports often provide faster/cheaper inland, while interior states add distance and time. Climate also affects things.

  • Proximity to port: Newark, Savannah, Jacksonville — optimal inland.
  • Interior states: Midwest — pickup takes longer; cost increases.
  • Seasonality: storms/holidays change logistics.
  • Inventory: big yards — more choice/competition.

By choosing the right location you often achieve better prices and timing.

FAQ

Can I avoid flood risk using only photos?

There is no 100% guarantee via photos, but strong signs are: water line/stains in the cabin, corrosion, smell/excess disinfectant, unusual electrical marks. VIN reports often record flood damage too.

Is “Run & Drive” enough?

No. It only means the car starts and moves in the yard. It does not rule out costly repairs, sensor issues, frame deformation or transmission faults.

What if I like two lots?

Set A/B priority; prepare all‑in caps for both; act with the right strategy on bidding day.

What’s the difference between Copart and IAAI?

Fee structures, inventory and yard policies differ somewhat. We compare daily and recommend the platform/lot that best fits your needs.

Is on‑site inspection possible?

For high‑value lots — yes, but the cost/time should be proportional. By default we rely on in‑depth remote analysis.
